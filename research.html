Research

My research has ranged from questions about gauge symmetries in curved spacetime to the thermodynamic cost of statistical inference. My longer-term interests are in quantum gravity and holography.

University of Pennsylvania · Feb 2026–present · Jonathan J. Heckman

Soft photon relations in de Sitter spacetime

My senior thesis research investigates whether the relation between soft photon theorems and generalized one-form symmetries in flat-space QED extends to late-time observables in de Sitter spacetime. I have studied Schwinger–Keldysh Ward identities, the interpretation of candidate soft charges, and curved-spacetime effective descriptions of charged particles. An ongoing question is whether soft gauge interactions factorize into Wilson lines in this setting.

University of Pennsylvania · Mar 2025–May 2026 · Andrea J. Liu and Adam Gabriel Frim

Optimal statistical inference under thermodynamic constraints

How should a physical memory update its beliefs when information processing dissipates energy? I developed a stochastic-thermodynamic model of sequential inference using finite-state memories, deriving a dissipation metric from Markov dynamics and studying optimal update protocols under accuracy and time constraints. A manuscript is in preparation.

University of Pennsylvania · Apr 2024–Mar 2025 · Dylan S. Rankin

Machine learning for particle physics

Developed a vector-quantized variational autoencoder for unsupervised anomaly detection in ATLAS particle-collision data, and trained classifiers on quantized latent representations to assess signal retention without reconstructing detector inputs.

Arizona State University · Jan 2022–Mar 2024 · Richard A. Kirian

Computational methods for XFEL experiments

Developed Python tools for characterizing gas dynamic virtual nozzles, including a machine-learning classifier for drip–jet flow regimes, and contributed particle image velocimetry and statistical analysis of liquid microjets.
